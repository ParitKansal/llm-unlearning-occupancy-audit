Prior-Work Analysis Report: Ecological Occupancy Modeling for Large Language Model Unlearning Audits
Verdict
NOT FOUND
A comprehensive prior-work audit across arXiv, OpenReview (ICLR, ICML, NeurIPS, TMLR), ACL Anthology (ACL, EMNLP, NAACL, EACL), Semantic Scholar, Google Scholar, LessWrong/Alignment Forum, and primary ecology/statistics journals (Methods in Ecology and Evolution, Ecology, Biometrics, JASA, Annals of Applied Statistics) confirms that no published or preprinted work applies ecological site-occupancy models (such as MacKenzie et al., 2002, or Royle & Nichols, 2003) or zero-inflated latent-presence models to large language model (LLM) unlearning, knowledge probing, or knowledge editing1.
While existing unlearning research explores probabilistic output distribution bounds (Scholten et al., 2025)3, empirical sampling leak metrics (Reisizadeh et al., 2025)6, dynamic multi-prompt adversarial search (Rybak et al., 2026; Tran To et al., 2025)7, internal representation information decomposition (Goel et al., 2026)4, and species-richness extrapolation (Li et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2026)5, no existing methodology formulates an unlearning forget set as a population of latent sites  with occupancy probabilities , while modeling diverse probe modalities (such as paraphrases, translations, cloze prompts, few-shot queries, temperature sampling, adversarial prefixes, and short relearning nudges) as repeated diagnostic visits  with probe-specific detection probabilities 1.
Literature Analysis Across Research Axes
Probabilistic Evaluation Frameworks and Sampling Bounds
Traditional evaluations of unlearning rely on deterministic point estimates generated via greedy decoding (), treating a fact as removed if a single prompt fails to elicit the target response3. Scholten et al. (2025) address the fragility of point-estimate evaluations by introducing a probabilistic evaluation framework for LLM unlearning and alignment3. Utilizing Monte Carlo sampling under multinomial decoding, Scholten et al. derive non-parametric Clopper-Pearson concentration bounds to establish upper bounds on binary leakage () and general expected leakage ()3. However, this approach evaluates the generation variance of a model's output distribution for individual prompts rather than decoupling latent fact retention () from variable probe detection probabilities () across distinct prompt structures or attack modalities3.
Expanding on sampling-based evaluation, Reisizadeh et al. (2025) introduce the leak@k metric, which measures the probability that at least one response out of  stochastic decoding samples leaks unlearned information6. Their empirical findings demonstrate that standard unlearning algorithms frequently suppress top-token generation while leaving target facts accessible in the sampling distribution tail6. Despite exposing surface-level unlearning flaws, leak@k functions strictly as an empirical attack success metric under repeated stochastic sampling6. It does not establish a parametric latent-variable model, nor does it separate false negatives caused by weak probes from genuine knowledge erasure6.
Adversarial Multi-Probe Stress-Testing and Knowledge Recovery
A major focus within unlearning evaluation centers on dynamic probe generation to test whether suppressed knowledge can be recovered under targeted adversarial pressure7. Rybak et al. (2026) propose REBEL, an evolutionary evaluation loop that pairs a hacker model with a judge model to iteratively mutate benign queries into role-play and hypothetical prompts, recovering up to 93% of unlearned biosecurity knowledge7. Similarly, Tran To et al. (2025) present LURK, which adapts Greedy Coordinate Gradient (GCG) search to optimize token suffixes that induce unlearned models to complete canonical domain facts8.
Both REBEL and LURK illustrate that single-probe evaluations yield high false-negative rates regarding knowledge removal7. However, these frameworks operate as heuristic stress-testing optimization algorithms designed to establish empirical lower bounds on attack success rates7. They do not provide statistical estimators, dataset-level confidence intervals for unobserved residual knowledge prevalence, or sample-size power analyses to determine auditing completeness7.
Internal Activation Decomposition and Representation Probing
Moving beyond output text generation, recent research investigates internal hidden layer representations to assess post-unlearning residual traces4. Goel et al. (2026) propose an auditing framework grounded in Partial Information Decomposition (PID), dissecting the joint mutual information between pre-unlearning base models and post-unlearning target models into uniquely erased information () and redundant residual knowledge ()4.
While Goel et al. successfully isolate internal memory traces from surface-level output suppression, their framework relies on full white-box access to model activations and linear probe classifiers4. In contrast, an ecological occupancy framework models behavioral detection dynamics directly from input-output observations, parameterizing probe-level non-detection probabilities () and fact-level latent presence () without requiring model parameter or activation access2.
Species-Richness and Capture-Recapture Frameworks in LLM Evaluation
The application of statistical ecology principles to language models has gained momentum through species-richness estimation and capture-recapture theory5. Li et al. (2025) introduce KnowSum, adapting Good-Turing and Orlitsky-style unseen-species estimators to extrapolate the total volume of unobserved knowledge (e.g., mathematical theorems) known by an LLM beyond a test set5. Liu et al. (2026) employ a Bayesian capture-recapture ecology framework to estimate the diversity and population size of an LLM's generative idea space for novelty assessment9. Additionally, Xin et al. (2026) develop UCS, which applies Smoothed Good-Turing estimation over discretized latent code clusters to optimize demonstration coverage in in-context learning15.
These works adapt species-richness estimation—extrapolating the total count of unobserved unique items in an open population based on sample occurrence frequencies5. The proposed research idea instead adapts site-occupancy modeling (MacKenzie et al., 2002), which operates on a closed set of specific unlearning targets ("sites") and models latent binary presence () vs. non-detection () across repeated heterogeneous diagnostic attempts ("visits")1.
Ecological Occupancy Models in Machine Learning Literature
An examination of machine learning literature referencing occupancy models reveals that ecological occupancy frameworks are utilized strictly as downstream statistical targets for environmental monitoring or building systems engineering2. Machine learning classifiers (such as Convolutional Neural Networks or bioacoustic classifiers) are paired with false-positive or continuous-score occupancy models to correct classifier errors when estimating biological species distributions2. Other applications include BuildOcc, a platform simulating human building occupant agents18, and InFOM, a reinforcement learning method modeling long-horizon state occupancy trajectories21. No prior research utilizes ecological site-occupancy models as a meta-evaluation methodology to assess machine learning model capabilities, unlearning efficacy, or knowledge probing robustness1.

Strongest Reviewer Objections and Methodological Counter-Evidence
A critical review of the proposed framework highlights a primary theoretical objection concerning model misspecification and statistical assumptions. Standard ecological site-occupancy models assume that site occupancy () represents a static binary latent state—where a species is strictly present or absent—and that repeated detection visits () possess conditionally independent observation errors1. In neural language models, knowledge removal post-unlearning rarely manifests as discrete binary deletion4. Fine-tuning modifications degrade weight associations along a continuous spectrum of association strengths4. Furthermore, diagnostic probes (such as paraphrases, translations, cloze formats, and short relearning nudges) share underlying semantic embeddings and attention routing pathways, inherently violating the assumption of visit independence4. Reviewers will argue that fitting a binary occupancy model to continuous, correlated knowledge degradation will yield misspecified parameter estimates and overly optimistic confidence intervals.
Addressing this objection requires establishing three empirical and theoretical validations within the proposed evaluation framework:
First, the estimator must undergo ground-truth calibration against a model retrained strictly from scratch on retain data (), where the target facts () were never observed4. Demonstrating that the occupancy estimator converges to  (with confidence intervals spanning zero) on the retrained reference model proves that baseline generation noise, partial prompt overlap, or commonsense guessing do not cause false-positive occupancy inflation4.

Third, estimated occupancy parameters () must be validated against physical relearning resistance4. By subjecting unlearned models to fine-tuning recovery attacks and recording the exact number of gradient steps  required to restore complete recall for each fact , the framework can demonstrate a strong monotonic correlation between estimated latent occupancy  and relearning vulnerability4.
